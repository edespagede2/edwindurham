Getting in is a discipline, not a talent

I was never the one being chased — I was the rep on the outside, learning what it actually takes to reach the person who can say yes. Forty-five years of that, across banks, telecom, utilities, and retail, taught me it isn't a personality trait. It's a method, and it works in any industry, at a company of any size.

The mix

Art and science, not a script

There's no single formula that opens every door. Getting through takes pattern recognition sharpened over decades, paired with real process — channel, message, timing, and follow-through, tracked and refined like any other discipline.

Stay current

The tools change every year — the goal doesn't

Whatever channel a gatekeeper hasn't yet learned to filter is usually the one that works. That means staying current on new platforms and integrations, and retiring an approach the moment it stops earning attention.

Or don't

Sometimes the oldest tool wins

New isn't always right. I once got through a door that email, phone, and LinkedIn couldn't touch by sending a telegram — long after telegrams had stopped being a serious business tool. It reached top executives at the company that pioneered dense wavelength-division multiplexing and laid much of the groundwork for today's broadband fiber-optic backbone. If it's the one thing on their desk they'll actually open, it's the right tool.
